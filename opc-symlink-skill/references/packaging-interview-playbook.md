# Packaging Interview Playbook

Use this playbook after scanning workspace context. The goal is to package an
AI builder style OPC for public promotion, not to collect a neutral biography.

## Opening Move

Start with a packaging hypothesis:

- "From the workspace, I think your strongest public angle is..."
- "The product signal I see is..."
- "The buyer or collaborator is still fuzzy..."
- "Before I sharpen this, what should I correct or keep private?"

Ask the user to correct the hypothesis before going deeper.

## Interview Stages

### Positioning

Goal: decide how a stranger should understand the user in one sentence.

Ask:

- "Should visitors see you primarily as an AI product builder, automation
  partner, technical founder, delivery consultant, or something sharper?"
- "What should people remember after 10 seconds on the page?"
- "What kind of work do you not want this page to attract?"

### Audience

Goal: find the best-fit visitor.

Ask:

- "Who do you most want to attract right now?"
- "What situation are they in when they need you?"
- "Are they buyers, partners, users, investors, collaborators, or followers?"

### Pain

Goal: name the audience's problem in their language.

Ask:

- "What are they currently doing manually, slowly, expensively, or badly?"
- "What do they already know they need, and what do they not know yet?"
- "What would make them say, 'I should talk to this person'?"

### Transformation

Goal: package the user's value as a before-and-after.

Ask:

- "What can you help them do that they could not do before?"
- "What changes after working with you or using your product?"
- "What is your mechanism: agents, workflows, product systems, training,
  integrations, or strategic judgment?"

### Offer

Goal: decide what the page should sell or invite.

Ask:

- "Is the strongest offer a product, advisory call, delivery project, training,
  co-building partnership, or long-term operator role?"
- "What should a qualified visitor ask you for?"
- "What should the primary CTA say?"

### Proof

Goal: make the packaging believable.

Ask:

- "Which public projects, demos, posts, repos, launches, or customer outcomes
  can we show?"
- "Are there metrics, named customers, testimonials, or collaborations that are
  approved for public use?"
- "If proof is early, what can we truthfully show as current work or direction?"

### Boundary

Goal: prevent accidental disclosure.

Ask:

- "Which customers, numbers, product details, or personal details must stay
  private?"
- "Should any claim be softened because it is still early?"
- "What should the page avoid promising?"

## Stop Condition

Stop interviewing when you can write:

- A one-sentence positioning line.
- A clear target audience and pain.
- A before-and-after transformation.
- At least one offer or product.
- At least one proof item or honest substitute.
- A primary CTA.
- A privacy boundary.

Then summarize the public packaging direction for user confirmation. Do not ask
the user to approve JSON unless they explicitly request it.
